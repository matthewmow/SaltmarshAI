How it gets built

The same rules apply to every project, because they're what make people willing to rely on an AI tool.

  • Answers come from your content

    If the assistant can't point to an approved source, it says so and offers a person. It never makes up a fact about your organization.

  • Privacy is designed in

    Consent is checked in code, not only in a prompt. Student and customer data stays where your policies say it can live, FERPA included.

  • Tested before every release

    Each tool ships with a set of real questions and expected answers. Accuracy and safety tests have to pass before anything goes live.

  • You choose who runs it

    Saltmarsh can host and maintain it for you, or your IT team can run it in your own environment. Either way, greetings, content, and routing are settings your staff change themselves.

In development, launching 2027

An enrollment assistant for a health-professions university

Prospective students arrive from ads with questions about programs, cost, and schedules, and many want answers before they're ready to talk to anyone. A packaged chatbot couldn't integrate with the university's systems the way they needed, so they chose a build designed around them.

What it does
Answers prospects' questions from the university's approved content with a source link, learns what program they're considering, and books a call with an admissions advisor.
What staff get
An admin portal with leads, transcripts, and the questions prospects ask most, plus settings they manage themselves.
How it's held to account
It always says it's an AI, never promises admission or aid, and must pass automated accuracy and safety tests before each release.
